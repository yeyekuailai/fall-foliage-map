# Autumn Atlas: data provenance and limitations

Updated: 2026-09-18. Coverage: contiguous United States.

## Tree groups and historical abundance (current visualization)

The map now uses ten independent US Forest Service FHTET modeled basal-area layers, filtered to `year = 2002 AND category = 1`. Official source: https://imagery.geoplatform.gov/iipp/rest/services/Vegetation/USFS_EDW_FHP_TreeSpeciesMetrics_BasalArea/ImageServer . Its description dates the modeled forest conditions to approximately 2002, using FIA plot measurements, satellite imagery, terrain, soils and climate. This is a historical modeled landscape, not a current tree census. Variables and source-file SHA-256 hashes are in forest-meta.json.

Source rasters are 30 m. For a lightweight website, ten unstyled U16 TIFF exports use a common WGS84 grid, 1200 × 520 pixels, bbox [-126,24,-66,50], 0.05° (about 4–6 km). Export resampling is bilinear; these are sampled display values, NOT area means. No-data is zero; sparse GeoTIFF blocks are read with rasterio, not treated as valid pixel values. A Gaussian sigma 0.65 display pixels softens sampling discontinuities. All species share ONE scaling constant (99.7th percentile of all positive values), clipped and quantized to 0–255. There is no independent per-species contrast stretch. The lossless grayscale PNG transports the ten numeric bands side by side in species.json order. Reproduce with scripts/build-forest.py (numpy, scipy, Pillow, rasterio; downloads public data using curl).

Tree sprites are representative groups. Their spatial sampling is deterministic within each zoom level: screen-density lattice jitter is anchored in map coordinates, inclusion probability increases monotonically with summed relative abundance, and species selection is weighted by those same abundance values. Changing date keeps placements stable. Panning does not rerandomize trees; changing scale or display-quality resamples the representative groups. Trees per screen are capped at 4500 (desktop) / 2400 (narrow screens). Counts are NOT physical trees per acre, and the shares are only among these ten species, not the entire forest. Empty areas may contain conifers, other species, no-data, or sparse occurrences omitted by the display threshold.

Ten original image-generated tree sprites are botanical-style artwork with separate crown silhouettes. They are not photographs or taxonomic identification material. Runtime chroma-keying removes the asset background; cached leaf tinting preserves shading and trunks. A seeded ±3.5-day visual variation avoids uniform color blocks, and sparse crown transparency at late stages indicates leaf loss. These small within-group differences and tree sizes are artistic, not measured. Tree positions are explicitly schematic. The familiar species-specific seasonal models below still control the colors.

Historical Little/USGS native-range maps and their metadata remain in the source archive for provenance, but the former continuous raster overlay is no longer rendered or downloaded by the page.

## Seasonal color: version 3 observation reference

Seven species now use Harvard Forest HF003 (v37): sugar maple, red maple, paper birch, quaking aspen, northern red oak, black tupelo and American beech. Sweetgum, tulip tree and bigleaf maple retain their original uncalibrated priors. The seven calibrations derive from ONE site around 42.535 N, 72.185 W, 350 m. None is nationally validated. Quaking aspen only has 10 eligible years through 2001; six other species have 32–33 eligible annual pairs through 2023.

Citation: O’Keefe J, VanScoy G. 2024. Phenology of Woody Species at Harvard Forest since 1990. Harvard Forest Data Archive: HF003 (v.37). Environmental Data Initiative. https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/bc5d2c15df4fa81aeadcd59ed7580c91 . License: CC0-1.0. The accompanying CSV files preserve the downloaded source. SHA-256 hashes and original URLs are in calibration.json. Source page: https://harvardforest1.fas.harvard.edu/exist/apps/datasets/showData.html?id=hf003 . The narrative metadata states fall 1992 was excepted, while the published annual table includes 1992 entries; the analysis uses the published table as supplied rather than inventing corrections.

Reproduce with `python scripts/calibrate-phenology.py` from the source checkout (numpy required). Tests: `node scripts/test-phenology.cjs`.

1. Published annual species-level 50% color-change and 50% leaf-fall day-of-year estimates are converted to days since September 1 OF EACH OBSERVATION YEAR, avoiding leap-year bias. A year is retained only if both dates are numeric and within the broad August–December autumn window. Annual species estimates are weighted equally; trees are not treated as independent annual climate replicates.
2. A chronological holdout uses the last seven years when 20+ years are available, otherwise the last three. The predictor is the median of TRAINING YEARS ONLY, separately for the two events. Absolute date error and mean signed color-date error are computed on held-out years. This validates a constant historical baseline at one site, not a weather-informed model, cross-site transfer, logistic curve shape, or the 2026 season. No model selection used the holdout.
3. After evaluation, reference centers are refit descriptively using all retained annual dates. Color and fall are separate logistic processes. Their widths are estimated from raw tree/year 10–90% transitions using first bracketed linear crossings, a cumulative maximum to regularize non-monotone field assessments, a maximum 21-day bracket gap, and valid widths of 2–80 days. Widths are first median-aggregated by year and then across years, and divided by 2*ln(9) for logistic scale. Width calibration uses full historical data; date-holdout scores do NOT validate curve shape.
4. The empirical 10th–90th percentiles shown in the UI describe historical annual 50% color dates AT THE REFERENCE SITE. They are not forecast intervals or formal confidence bounds. The plotted green/gold points identify training/holdout years; the dashed line is the frozen training-median prediction.
5. Elsewhere, the reference dates are shifted by the existing species-specific latitude and elevation coefficients relative to the reference station. These transfer coefficients are ASSUMPTIONS, not estimated from HF003. All locations outside the reference area are explicitly described as unvalidated extrapolations. The UI shows the distance to the reference station. The map layer lacks a DEM and uses elevation 0; only a selected location with known elevation uses that value. Historical date calibration does not calibrate abundance, individual-tree color, or optical reflectance.
6. Continuous color progress is 0.8*logistic(color-change fraction) + 0.2*logistic(leaf-fall fraction). This is a visual combination, not an observed spectral metric or proof of peak viewing conditions. The five-color palettes retain botanical descriptions from NC State Extension. Each calibrated tree separately exposes modeled 50% coloration and 50% leaf fall rather than equating these to peak scenic color.
7. The remaining three species use the previous authored linear seasonal priors and explicitly show no fabricated validation metrics. The settings switch allows comparison to the original uncalibrated model for all species.

No current weather adjustment, environmental training, national cross-validation, or 2026 observation assimilation is included. Seven-day weather remains independent. A calibrated historical baseline is not a current-season forecast.

## City detail

GBIF API queries selected taxa, country=US, coordinates present, occurrenceStatus=PRESENT, and no flagged geospatial issues within the visible viewport. Selected species are batched into one API request, limited to 300 returned records per viewport. Selecting one species gives it the full query budget. Exact 5-decimal coordinate duplicates within a species are collapsed. This is a bounded API sample, not a systematic survey. Absence of records does not establish species absence. Records may be historical, cultivated or spatially uncertain. Popups disclose date, coordinate uncertainty, record source and the original GBIF record/individual licensing. Marker color is model output and does not describe observed foliage. GBIF point coordinates are never procedurally invented. They are separate from the new representative sprite groups, which are procedurally placed. The GBIF overlay is off by default and requested only when enabled at city zoom, reducing load.

GBIF API: https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/openapi/v1/occurrence

## Weather and external maps

Open-Meteo current modeled conditions and seven-day forecasts are fetched for the selected location, cached for ten minutes, and labeled with provider time and retrieval time. These are independent of the seasonal timeline. Attribution: Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0; free API for non-commercial prototype use. https://open-meteo.com/en/docs

City search: an offline Natural Earth city index plus on-demand Open-Meteo / GeoNames results. City and state are parsed separately (full state names and postal abbreviations); exact names rank before prefixes, with population as a tie-breaker. Known aliases include NYC, LA, SF and DC. Remote requests include a canonical state qualifier and countryCode=US. Airports, parks, abandoned settlements and city neighborhoods are excluded by feature code. Nearby city/state duplicates are merged, preferring GeoNames coordinates. Each result shows its state; selection is explicit. Clearing or dismissing a query aborts it and invalidates pending results. Coverage is restricted to the contiguous U.S. to match the map. https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/geocoding-api

NWS: location-specific links to https://forecast.weather.gov/MapClick.php . NWS data are not silently substituted for Open-Meteo.

Explore Fall original-page iframe: https://www.explorefall.com/fall-foliage-map . This is an external website view, not a local forecast feed; its timeline and displayed year are independent. Availability is determined by the provider. Original source links also include https://smokymountains.com/fall-foliage-map and https://www.usanpn.org/data . No third-party forecast data is ingested into the local model. The SmokyMountains official embed was tested but rendered blank with a d3 runtime error, so it was replaced and its original-site link retained.

Google Maps: official Maps URLs search and directions integration, no API key. https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started . Google basemap/Places API is not used.

Basemap: locally served Natural Earth state boundaries, populated place labels and generalized urban-area outlines (public domain, https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). Urban polygons show approximate built-up areas, not municipal boundaries. They are simplified to 0.004 degrees and shown from zoom 7; labels are culled to avoid collisions. Source files: https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/tree/master/geojson (ne_10m_populated_places_simple.geojson and ne_10m_urban_areas.geojson). Reproduce with scripts/build-city-map.py. Zoom is capped at 10 for city overview. There are NO external raster tile requests; Leaflet 1.9.4 is used only for pan/zoom and coordinate projection. Basemap, sprite atlas and numeric forest data are local. Weather, geocoding, external forecasts and GBIF are loaded only on request.
